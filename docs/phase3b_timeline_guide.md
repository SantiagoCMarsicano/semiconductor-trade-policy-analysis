# Phase 3B — Evidence-based timeline guide, 2014–2026

**Cut-off date: 14 November 2026**

This guide closes the historical/documentary stage of Semiconductors II. It does **not** select causal treatment dates automatically. The policy chronology was constructed after the Phase 3A endogenous breakpoint analysis.

## Reading convention

- **BACKGROUND** — industrial-policy or strategic prehistory.
- **TRADE WAR** — reciprocal tariff conflict.
- **TECH WAR** — restrictions on firms, technology, equipment, know-how or access.
- **INDUSTRIAL POLICY** — subsidies, domestic-capacity strategy or supply-chain policy.
- **CONFOUNDER** — major non-policy market shock.
- **OUR RESULT** — endogenous breakpoint estimated by Semiconductors II.
- **CURRENT STATE** — post-sample institutional context.

---

## 2014–2016 — Industrial-policy prehistory

### 26 Jun 2014 — China IC Industry Development Guidelines — BACKGROUND
China deepens a national strategy for integrated circuits.

**Evidence**
- Primary: Chinese official policy documentation.
- Academic: Bown & Wang (2024), *Journal of Economic Perspectives* 38(4):81–110, doi:10.1257/jep.38.4.81.

**Thesis interpretation**
This belongs to the industrial-policy prehistory of the rivalry. It is **not** the beginning of a bilateral U.S.–China trade war.

### 2014 — Big Fund I — INDUSTRIAL POLICY
Creation of a large state-backed semiconductor investment vehicle.

### 8 May 2015 — Made in China 2025 — BACKGROUND / INDUSTRIAL POLICY
Broader advanced-manufacturing strategy with important semiconductor implications.

**Academic comparison**
Bown & Wang (2024) place Chinese semiconductor support within the broader return of modern industrial policy.

### 2015–2016 — OUR RESULT
WSTS Asia Pacific and Worldwide log-trend breakpoints appear around **Dec 2015**; the China 3MMA layer later shows a candidate break around **Dec 2016**.

**Interpretation**
The historical background is relevant, but no single 2014–2016 policy is assigned as the cause.

## 2017–2019 — From Section 301 to reciprocal trade war and technology war

### 18 Aug 2017 — Section 301 investigation initiated — TRADE-WAR PRELUDE
USTR formally opens an investigation into Chinese practices related to technology transfer, intellectual property and innovation.

**Primary**
USTR: https://ustr.gov/about-us/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2017/august/ustr-announces-initiation-section

**Academic**
Wu (2024), *The Chinese Journal of International Politics* 17(4):323–345, doi:10.1093/cjip/poae017.

**Dating convention**
Administrative/political prelude, not yet the effective start of reciprocal tariff warfare.

### 6 Jul 2018 — First reciprocal tariff tranche — TRADE WAR
U.S. additional tariffs of **25%** take effect on **818 tariff lines covering approximately USD 34 billion** of Chinese imports. USTR also documents Chinese retaliation on approximately USD 34 billion of U.S. exports.

**Primary**
- USTR: https://ustr.gov/about-us/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2018/june/ustr-issues-tariffs-chinese-products
- USTR: https://ustr.gov/about-us/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2018/july/ustr-releases-product-exclusion

**Academic**
- Fajgelbaum, Goldberg, Kennedy & Khandelwal (2020), *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 135(1):1–55, doi:10.1093/qje/qjz036.
- Amiti, Redding & Weinstein (2019), *Journal of Economic Perspectives* 33(4):187–210, doi:10.1257/jep.33.4.187.
- Wu (2024), doi:10.1093/cjip/poae017.

**Thesis dating convention**
**Preferred effective start of the reciprocal U.S.–China trade war.**

### 23 Aug 2018 — List 2 — TRADE WAR
Approximately USD 16 billion of additional Chinese imports become subject to 25% tariffs.

**Primary**
USTR: https://ustr.gov/about-us/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2018/august/ustr-finalizes-second-tranche

### 24 Sep 2018 — List 3 — TRADE WAR
A much larger tariff tranche takes effect, intensifying the conflict.

### Nov–Dec 2018 — OUR RESULT
Estimated primary log-trend breakpoints:
- America: **Nov 2018**
- Asia Pacific: **Nov 2018**
- Worldwide: **Nov 2018**
- Japan: **Dec 2018**
- China 3MMA: **Dec 2018**

**Interpretation**
The temporal proximity to tariff escalation is important, but the simultaneous semiconductor-cycle deterioration is a major competing explanation.

### 16 May 2019 — Huawei Entity List — TECH WAR
Huawei and many affiliates are placed on the Entity List, restricting access to items subject to U.S. export controls.

**Primary**
BIS Huawei FAQ/documentation.

**Academic framing**
Wu (2024) analytically distinguishes the tariff-based trade war from a technology war centered on restrictions on Chinese access to U.S. technology.

**Specialist**
Bown (2020), *How the United States marched the semiconductor industry into its trade war with China*, PIIE.

**Thesis dating convention**
May 2019 is a defensible date for the **consolidation of a distinct technology-war phase**, while acknowledging important earlier technology-security measures.

## 2020–2021 — Pandemic, shortage and technological decoupling

### 11 Mar 2020 — COVID-19 pandemic — CONFOUNDER
Pandemic shock alters demand composition, production, logistics and capacity allocation.

**Thesis treatment**
Never code COVID as part of the U.S.–China policy treatment. It is a central competing explanation.

### 17 Aug 2020 — Huawei FDPR expansion — TECH WAR
U.S. controls extend more deeply into foreign-produced chips made using specified U.S. technology.

**Academic / specialist comparison**
Bown (2020) explains the supply-chain chokepoint logic of U.S. semiconductor export restrictions.

### 2021 — Semiconductor shortage and record sales — CONFOUNDER
High demand and supply constraints coexist. Semiconductor shortage does not mechanically imply declining semiconductor revenues.

### 24 Feb 2021 — Executive Order 14017 — INDUSTRIAL / SUPPLY-CHAIN POLICY
Semiconductors become an explicit U.S. supply-chain-security priority.

### 2021 — OUR RESULT
Important candidate changes appear in Japan, China and other smoothed series around 2021.

**Interpretation**
COVID recovery, shortage dynamics, Chinese self-reliance and U.S. supply-chain policy overlap strongly; single-cause interpretations are especially weak.

## 2022 — Industrial-policy revival and semiconductor chokepoints

### 9 Aug 2022 — CHIPS and Science Act — INDUSTRIAL POLICY
Major U.S. semiconductor manufacturing and R&D support.

**Academic**
- Bown & Wang (2024), *Journal of Economic Perspectives*.
- Erten, Stiglitz & Verhoogen (2025 BPEA / 2026 NBER WP 34625) find positive local semiconductor and construction employment effects and evidence of anticipatory responses before final enactment.

**Implication**
A single August 2022 dummy could miss anticipation effects.

### Sep 2022 — OUR RESULT
Asia Pacific and Worldwide WSTS log-trend breakpoints are estimated around **Sep 2022**.

### 7 Oct 2022 — Advanced-computing / semiconductor-manufacturing controls — TECH WAR
BIS restricts Chinese access to certain advanced computing chips, supercomputing capabilities and advanced semiconductor manufacturing.

**Primary**
BIS: https://www.bis.gov/press-release/commerce-implements-new-export-controls-advanced-computing-semiconductor-manufacturing-items-peoples

**Academic comparison**
Bown & Wang (2024) and later empirical export-control studies.

**Critical causal caution**
Because our Asia Pacific/Worldwide breakpoint is estimated around September 2022, the formal 7 Oct rule cannot simply be declared the cause. Anticipation, prior actions and the semiconductor cycle must be examined.

## 2023–2024 — Multilateral controls, Chinese responses and renewed industrial policy

### 2023 — Allied equipment controls — TECH WAR
The Netherlands and Japan introduce or implement controls affecting advanced semiconductor manufacturing equipment.

**Evidence basis**
- Official and institutional documentation of allied export-control measures.
- The 2025 CRS semiconductor-export-control report provides an official synthesis of the broader allied-control architecture.

**Thesis use**
Treat allied controls as part of the widening technology-control regime, while avoiding claims about their quantitative effects unless supported by reviewed empirical literature.

### 1 Aug 2023 — Chinese gallium / germanium controls — ECONOMIC-SECURITY RESPONSE
China activates export licensing for selected critical materials.

### 17 Oct 2023 — U.S. updates 2022 controls — TECH WAR
BIS strengthens advanced-computing and semiconductor-manufacturing restrictions.

### 2023 — OUR RESULT
- China log trend: **Jul 2023**
- China share trend: **Nov 2023**
- AP Other: around **Dec 2023**

**Interpretation**
These breaks occur amid controls, adaptation, market normalization and Chinese industrial responses; they are not assigned to a single event.

### 2024 — Big Fund III — INDUSTRIAL POLICY
China renews large-scale state-backed semiconductor financing.

### 13 Sep 2024 — USTR Section 301 modifications — TRADE / INDUSTRIAL POLICY
Strategic-sector tariff increases are finalized, including a higher semiconductor tariff scheduled for 2025.

### 2 Dec 2024 — further BIS controls — TECH WAR
Additional semiconductor-manufacturing-equipment, software/HBM and Entity List restrictions.

## 2025–2026 — Recent evidence actually reviewed

### 2025 — Corporate resilience under U.S. export controls
**He et al. (2025), PLOS ONE**

Using Chinese listed manufacturing firms and a multi-period difference-in-differences design, the study finds that exposure to U.S. export controls is associated with weaker corporate resilience through channels including internationalization and financing conditions.

**Use**
Evidence that export restrictions can impose measurable firm-level costs even when aggregate semiconductor-market effects are more ambiguous.

### 2025 — Export controls and domestic innovation responses
**Liu et al. (2025), Harvard Business School Working Paper 25-004**

The paper studies an earlier U.S. export-control regime and finds increases in R&D spending, patenting and active inventors among affected Chinese firms.

**Use**
Mechanism evidence that technology restrictions can induce domestic innovation responses. It is not direct evidence about the October 2022 semiconductor controls.

### 2025 / 2026 — Costs to U.S. suppliers
**Crosignani et al., Federal Reserve Bank of New York Staff Report; later published in Journal of Financial Economics**

The study finds that affected U.S. technology suppliers reduce sales to Chinese customers but also experience losses in market capitalization and declines in profitability, employment and lending, while Chinese firms reconfigure supply chains.

**Use**
Evidence against a one-directional interpretation in which export controls impose costs only on Chinese firms.

### Interpretation
The reviewed literature supports a multidimensional view: technology restrictions can simultaneously impose costs, induce adaptation, alter innovation incentives and reconfigure supply chains.

No reviewed source is used to claim that these mechanisms necessarily translate one-for-one into WSTS regional semiconductor sales.

# Current institutional state — 14 Nov 2026

### 13 Jan 2026 — selective licensing adjustment — CURRENT STATE
BIS moves Nvidia H200, AMD MI325X and similar chips to **case-by-case licensing** under specified conditions.

Primary:
https://www.bis.gov/press-release/department-commerce-revises-license-review-policy-semiconductors-exported-china

**Interpretation**
Selective adjustment, not termination of the export-control regime.

### 14–15 Jan 2026 — Section 232 semiconductor action — CURRENT STATE
The United States adopts an immediate **25% tariff on a narrow category of certain advanced-computing chips**, with enumerated exemptions connected to U.S. domestic-capacity and technology uses.

Primary:
https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/01/adjusting-imports-of-semiconductors-semiconductor-manufacturing-equipment-and-their-derivative-products-into-the-united-states/

### Nov 2026 — Section 301 architecture remains active — CURRENT STATE
USTR extends **178 current product exclusions through 10 Jan 2027**.

**Interpretation**
The trade-policy architecture remains active even while exclusions and licensing rules are selectively adjusted.

### Recommended thesis wording
> As of November 2026, the U.S.–China semiconductor dispute is better characterized as institutionalized and managed economic-security competition than as a tariff war alone. Reciprocal tariff measures coexist with export controls, industrial policy, security screening and selective licensing adjustments.

This is a synthesis. Current factual components come from official documents; peer-reviewed literature is used to interpret accumulated mechanisms and effects.

# Final periodization for Semiconductors II

| Stage | Approximate period | Thesis label |
|---|---|---|
| Chinese industrial-policy buildup | 2014–2017 | **Industrial-policy prehistory** |
| Section 301 investigation / escalation | Aug 2017–H1 2018 | **Trade-war formation** |
| Reciprocal tariffs | Jul 2018–2019 | **Trade war** |
| Huawei / supply-chain technology restrictions | May 2019–2021 | **Technology-war consolidation** |
| Advanced-chip and SME controls | Oct 2022–2024 | **Semiconductor chokepoint regime** |
| Adjustment + persistent controls | 2025–2026 | **Managed economic-security competition** |

## Three dates to remember

**18 Aug 2017** — administrative/political beginning of Section 301 escalation.

**6 Jul 2018** — preferred effective start of the reciprocal trade war.

**16 May 2019 / 7 Oct 2022** — successive landmarks in the technology war: firm-centered restrictions and later broad semiconductor chokepoint controls.

# Relation to our econometric evidence

The chronology does not retroactively determine the breakpoints.

The main comparisons to carry into Phase 3C are:

1. **Late 2018:** tariff escalation versus semiconductor-cycle deterioration.
2. **2021:** shortage/COVID normalization versus supply-chain/security policy.
3. **2022–23:** CHIPS Act + October 2022 export controls versus global semiconductor-cycle normalization and Chinese adaptation.

These are competing mechanisms to test or control for, not stories to assume.
